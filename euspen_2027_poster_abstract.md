## Title (lower case)

quantum limits of surface metrology: spatial information extraction by a single trapped ion

## Abstract

A single trapped atomic ion is proposed and analysed as a non-contact electrostatic probe of conducting surfaces, and the fundamental limits of the spatial information it can extract are established. Confined in a radio-frequency Paul trap at height h above the surface, the ion responds to surface electrostatic perturbations through shifts of its secular motional frequency. Boundary-perturbation theory on Laplace's equation yields the instrument transfer function H(k; h) ∝ k_x^2 e^(-kh), a band-pass filter whose peak wavelength λ = πh scales linearly with standoff. The e^(-kh) factor imposes an irreversible spatial-information cutoff: at h = 40 µm only six independent Fourier modes are recoverable in one dimension, the effective rank scales as r_eff ∝ 1/h, and the resolution–height product equals h·Δk_FWHM = 3.395. A theorem is proved that multi-height scanning alone cannot separate surface topography from surface charge, because both contributions share the identical e^(-kh) scaling; in the realistic uncertainty budget this degeneracy yields a combined standard uncertainty of 319 nm, with surface charge contributing 99.99 % of the variance, while the measurement sensitivity itself reaches 1 nm at a 5 Hz technical floor (0.05 nm at the transfer-function peak), with a path to picometres through motional squeezing at the quantum projection noise limit: 13 dB of motional squeezing improves the amplitude sensitivity 4.5-fold, with the Heisenberg limit as the ideal bound under zero-decoherence conditions. The framework is validated against an exact Rayleigh (Floquet) electrostatic solver, independent boundary-element and finite-difference implementations, and published atomic-force-microscopy data of a stainless-steel surface, reconstructed with correlations improving from 0.62 to 0.76 as the height decreases from 40 µm to 5 µm. Five quantitative falsifiable experimental predictions are formulated, defining a benchmark for the planned experimental demonstration.
