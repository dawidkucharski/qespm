# Cover Letter — Physical Review Applied

Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript "Quantum Limits of Surface Metrology: Spatial Information Extraction by a Single Trapped Ion" for consideration as a Regular Article in Physical Review Applied.

**Context.** Trapped atomic ions are among the most sensitive electric-field detectors ever realised, but they have been used exclusively as qualitative electrometers — detecting the *presence* of surface fields — never as quantitative topographic imaging tools with a defined instrument transfer function, traceable calibration, and compliant uncertainty budget. This gap is addressed here.

**Key findings.** The paper derives a complete theoretical and metrological framework for surface topography measurement with a single trapped ion. Three results are of particular note:

1. The instrument transfer function $H_x(k;h) \propto k_x^2 e^{-kh}$ has a distinctive band-pass character, and the $e^{-kh}$ factor imposes a fundamental spatial-information cutoff that no quantum enhancement can circumvent — a result proved by factorising the quantum Fisher information as $\mathcal{F}_A(k;h) = |H_x|^2 \mathcal{F}_{\rm ion}$ and demonstrating numerically that $\lambda_{\min}$ is identical across classical, quantum-projection-noise, squeezed, and Heisenberg-limited measurement regimes.

2. A theorem establishes a topography–charge identifiability limit: multi-height scanning can never separate geometric from electrostatic surface features — a fundamental constraint shared by all non-contact electrostatic probes.

3. The framework is validated against published AFM data of stainless steel ($r = 0.62 \to 0.76$ as ion height decreases from 40 to 5 µm), against an independent boundary-element electrostatic solver, and against two analytical benchmarks (step-edge and Gaussian bump).

**Fit to Physical Review Applied.** The paper bridges fundamental quantum limits — the $e^{-kh}$ transfer function, the QFI × ITF factorisation, the identifiability theorem — with practical metrology: a GUM-compliant uncertainty budget (all six input quantities verified), a five-step SI-traceable calibration procedure, and a proposal for classification under ISO 25178-6. This dual physics–metrology character is the distinctive mandate of PRApplied.

**Submission history.** This manuscript has not been previously submitted to any journal.
