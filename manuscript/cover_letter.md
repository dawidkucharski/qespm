# Cover Letter — Surface Topography: Metrology and Properties (IOP)

Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript "Quantum Limits of Surface Metrology: Spatial Information Extraction by a Single Trapped Ion" for consideration as a research paper in Surface Topography: Metrology and Properties.  The submission comprises a 32-page main manuscript and a 30-page Supplementary Material.

**Context.** Trapped atomic ions are among the most sensitive electric-field detectors realised to date, but they have been used as qualitative electrometers — detecting the *presence* of surface fields — and, to the present knowledge, have not been formulated as quantitative topographic imaging tools with a defined instrument transfer function, regularised inversion and an uncertainty budget.  This gap is addressed here.

**Key findings.** The paper derives a complete theoretical and metrological framework for surface topography measurement with a single trapped ion.  Three results are of particular note:

1. The instrument transfer function $H_x(k;h) \propto k_x^2 e^{-kh}$ has a distinctive band-pass character, and the $e^{-kh}$ factor imposes a spatial-information cutoff that no quantum enhancement can circumvent — demonstrated by factorising the quantum Fisher information as $\mathcal{F}_A(k;h) = |H_x|^2 \mathcal{F}_{\rm ion}$ and showing that the effective rank and the 50\% MTF cutoff are identical across classical, quantum-projection-noise, squeezed and Heisenberg-limited regimes.

2. A theorem establishes a topography–charge identifiability limit: multi-height scanning alone cannot separate geometric from electrostatic surface features — a constraint shared by all non-contact electrostatic probes.

3. The first-order model is cross-checked against an exact Rayleigh–Floquet solver (0.01\% relative error at $A=100$\,nm, growing as $0.14\,(kA)^2$), an independent boundary-element solver ($\le0.6\%$) and a finite-difference solver, and the reconstruction chain is evaluated on published AFM topography of stainless steel (Pearson correlation improving from $r=0.62$ at $h=40$\,\textmu m to $r=0.78$ at $h=2$\,\textmu m); this evaluation uses experimentally measured topography as input and is not presented as an experimental validation of the instrument, whose demonstration remains future work.

**Metrological content.** The manuscript provides a GUM-based uncertainty budget in which the dominant contribution is the conditional, charge-limited reconstruction uncertainty ($u_c(z)=319$\,nm under the stated residual-charge baseline), a five-step SI-traceable calibration procedure, a comparison with established surface-metrology techniques, a proposal for classification under ISO~25178-6, and a phased experimental roadmap.

**Fit to Surface Topography: Metrology and Properties.** The paper is a metrology-framework contribution: measurand and influence-quantity definitions, an explicit instrument transfer function, an uncertainty budget following the GUM, ISO~25178 classification considerations, and falsifiable experimental predictions.  The distinction between intrinsic detection sensitivity and topographic reconstruction uncertainty — and the identifiability limit imposed by surface charge — speaks directly to the journal's readership in areal surface metrology.

**Data and code availability.** All analysis code and the synthetic benchmark dataset are publicly available in the GitHub repository (https://github.com/dawidkucharski/qespm) and archived in Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22721000).  The AFM topography used for the numerical evaluation is from Camargo Jr. (2019), Mendeley Data, CC BY 4.0 (https://doi.org/10.17632/6dzmrjngcg.3).  The corresponding Data Availability section appears in the manuscript.
