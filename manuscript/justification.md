# 100-Word Justification — Physical Review Applied

This paper addresses a question at the boundary of quantum physics and precision metrology: how much spatial information can a single quantum probe extract from a surface, and what are the irreducible physical limits? The instrument transfer function $H_x \propto k_x^2 e^{-kh}$ is derived from first-principles electrostatics and ion-trap dynamics. Its $e^{-kh}$ factor imposes a spatial-information cutoff that quantum enhancement — squeezed states, Heisenberg-limited interferometry — cannot circumvent, as proved by the factorisation $\mathcal{F}_A(k;h) = |H_x|^2 \mathcal{F}_{\rm ion}$ and a four-regime numerical comparison. The complete metrological framework — GUM budget, SI-traceable calibration, ISO 25178 classification proposal — connects these fundamental limits to practical surface measurement, consistent with PRApplied's mandate to bridge physics and technology.
